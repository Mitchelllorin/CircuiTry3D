What is ours, not yours

Time to open Your I²t predicts 0.5405 / 0.893² = 0.678 s at this overload. That is arithmetic on your figure, not a measurement — the model has no time axis for the melt.
The whole time-current curve Not modelled. The engine opens the element on a current threshold, so an inrush a real 218 would ride through will trip this one. This is the model's largest inaccuracy and it is stated as such wherever the part appears.
I²t as let-through energy Your 0.5405 A²s is carried and reported, but not integrated to produce a trip time. The figure is yours; the use of it is ours.
Interrupting behaviour The 35 A rating is carried as a rating. Arc quenching and the glass-body rupture are not simulated.
Failure name and severity "Fuse Element Melts", severity 3 of 3. F.U.S.E.'s judgement, marked in the record as the model's, never as a published characteristic.

The question

  1. What should the model do at 3.6×? A 218 is time-lag. If the answer is "it clears in about this long, and here is the curve", the model can hold it and the bench becomes worth something to a person sizing a fuse rather than only to a student watching one die.
  2. Which parts would you rather we carried? The 218 is one part in one family. A part you already consider misapplied is a more useful subject than one I chose.

Both are answerable in a reply. A correction goes into the model and is credited on the part's card.

Also carried

1812L110/16DR (resettable PPTC) and SMF5.0A (TVS diode). Both are carried as reference parts and marked reference-only in the model — the engine does not represent PPTC trip and reset, or transient clamping. Listed here so the answer to "what else of ours do you have" is a true one.
