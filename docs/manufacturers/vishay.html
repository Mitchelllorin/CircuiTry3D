CRCW e3, on a bench where a student watches it fail

Four values of your thick-film chip resistor are modelled from the series sheet and driven past their rating in a 3D circuit simulator used by electrical apprentices and their instructors. This page is the model, the figures it came from, and the one part of it I cannot check without you.

What the bench does with them

The part is placed on a board with a supply and a load, and the load is ramped until something gives. The console prints the rating and the derated rating side by side, at the ambient the bench is actually at, because above ambient the part cannot do what the headline number says:

What is ours, not yours

The 70 °C knee This is my reading of your curve, not a figure quoted from it. It replaced a 25 °C default that was telling students their resistor had less margin than your sheet gives it.
Thermal resistance 200 °C/W (0603) and 300 °C/W (0402) are the app's model constants. They are not your published figures and are not presented as such.
Failure names and severity "Thermal runaway", "open element" and the 0–3 severity are F.U.S.E.'s judgements. The record marks them as the model's, never as yours.
Temperature coefficient Carried as a family default unless a part declares its own. If the e3 series has a published figure worth using, it belongs in the model as yours.

The question

  1. Confirm the curve. Is it full rating to 70 °C and linear to zero at 155 °C for the e3 series, or does it depart from linear near the top?
  2. Which parts would you rather we carried? The resistor is the first thing every student builds, so it is the one that gets used most — but a part you would like future engineers to meet is a better use of the space than one I picked.

Both are answerable in a reply. A correction goes into the model and is credited on the part's card.
